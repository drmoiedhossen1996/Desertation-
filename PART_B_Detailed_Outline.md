### 1. INTRODUCTION (~1,500–2,000 words)

#### 1.1 Clinical Context: Aortic Stenosis and Pressure Overload
- AS prevalence, natural history, pressure overload → concentric hypertrophy
- Conventional indices (gradients, AVA, EF) limitations in capturing energetic burden
- **Category B** — Part A Ch.1, ESC/EACTS 2021 guidelines (Part A Ref [1])

#### 1.2 Myocardial Energetics Framework: Pressure–Volume Loops
- PV loops as reference framework: SW, PVA, efficiency (SW/PVA)
- Invasive PV-loop limitations → need for non-invasive alternatives
- **Category B** — Part A Ch.5, Knaapen 2007, Seemann 2019, Arvidsson 2023

#### 1.3 Non-Invasive CMR-Derived PV Loops: Principle and Validation
- CMR volumes + brachial BP + time-varying elastance model → non-invasive PV loops
- Validation status: HF populations (Arvidsson 2023); **no direct validation in AS**
- **Category B** — Part A Ch.5, Arvidsson 2023 (Part A Ref [19]), Seemann 2019 (Part A Ref [20])

#### 1.4 4D-Flow CMR Haemodynamic Energetics
- 4D-flow measures: KE, vorticity, viscous energy loss (VEL)
- LV intracavitary KE (Elhawaz 2021) vs aortic flow energy (Garcia 2019, Aliabadi 2025)
- **Critical distinction**: Flow energy ≠ myocardial mechanical work
- **Category B** — Part A Ch.4–5, Elhawaz 2021 (Part A Ref [17]), Garcia 2019 (Part A Ref [7]), Aliabadi 2025 (Part A Ref [8])

#### 1.5 Knowledge Gap
- No study has combined non-invasive PV-loop myocardial energetics with 4D-flow haemodynamic energetics in the same AS cohort
- PV-loop validation lacking in AS; 4D-flow energetics remain primarily research tools
- **Category B** — Part A Ch.5, Ch.7 discussion

#### 1.6 Study Rationale
- PREFER-CMR registry provides CMR + 4D-flow + BP dataset
- Opportunity to evaluate both myocardial (PV-loop) and flow (4D-flow) energetics in same patients
- **Category A/C** — Student's project rationale per Proposal and Reflective Essay

#### 1.7 Aim
> To evaluate myocardial energetic burden (via non-invasive CMR-derived PV loops) and 4D-flow haemodynamic energetics (KE, vorticity, energy loss) in patients with moderate-to-severe AS compared to age-matched healthy controls.
